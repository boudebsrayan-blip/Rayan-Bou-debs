## 2. Core philosophy [Bergles]

1. **Long-term over short-term.** "Not looking for short-term results, but to maximize where these
   young athletes are **3–5+ years down the line**." Short-term chasing sacrifices the final product.
2. **Peak at 17–18,** not at 12. A 6–7 year development pathway that starts in middle school.
3. **Make it fun and engaging** — especially with big groups of young kids. Engagement = progress.
   Shorter rotations with high engagement beat long, boring blocks.
4. **Technique before load.** Proper technique in the lifts over big numbers. Full range of motion.
5. **Simple things, done consistently and hard.** His "Training Simplified for 14–18 year olds":
   1. Squat deep with great technique
   2. Do full range-of-motion chin-ups
   3. Sprint
   4. Jump
   5. Work hard
   6. Be consistent
6. **Joint health / resilience first.** Known for "maintaining joint integrity and overall health"
   while increasing athletic potential — uses **Functional Range Conditioning (FRC)** principles.
7. **Athlete ownership.** Athletes are 100% responsible for recording their own measurements.
   Measurement exists to drive *intent*, not for the coach's spreadsheet.
8. **Life skills in the weight room** — discipline, showing up, coaching each other.
9. **Proof of the long game:** he posts kids like *8th grade 25.6" vertical → 10th grade 36.6"*:
   "I see a lot of 8th graders with 25+ inch verticals. I don't see a lot of 16–18 year olds jumping
   36+. This is what happens when you start training intelligently at a young age, work hard & are
   consistent."

## 3. Age-stage progression [Bergles + Applied]

| Age | Bergles' emphasis | What it looks like [Applied] |
|---|---|---|
| **7–10** (3rd–5th gr) | "Everything we do has to be fun." Movement fundamentals, coordination, balance | Games, tag/chase, skips, hops, crawls, throws, bodyweight squats/lunges, isometric holds, short sprints. No grinding. |
| **11–12** | "We can start to sprinkle in more." The **5 main movements** are the start point for 12-year-olds | Learn squat, hinge, push, pull, single-leg/lunge (+ carries/core) with bodyweight & light implements. Low-level plyos. Timed sprints for fun. |
| **13–14** (junior high) | "When we get to junior high, we have to start doing more." | Structured 2–3x/week lifting, goblet/front squat, RDL, chin-up progressions, split squats, plyo progressions, acceleration work, jump/sprint testing. |
| **15–18** (high school) | Squat deep, chin-ups, sprint, jump, work hard, be consistent. Goal = peak at 17–18 | Progressive loading (front squat, trap bar, cleans optional), max velocity work, advanced plyos (hurdle hops, depth jumps), more data. |

**Strength standard he cites:** every athlete he's had who could standing-vertical **40"+** could
**full front squat ~2x bodyweight** — no belt, no half squats. Strength is the foundation of jumping.

## 4. Key exercises he highlights [Bergles]

- **Front squat — full depth** (his marker lift for jumpers).
- **Full-ROM chin-ups.** He starts everyone with **isometrics and eccentrics**, then builds to full reps
  (flexed-arm hang → slow negatives → band/partial → full chin-ups).
- **Front-foot-elevated split squats** and **Cossack squat holds** — "2 movements every kid should be doing."
- **Isometrics** — uses them with kids as young as **8** and with pros (posts: "3 Isometric Drills I
  Use w/ Kids", "4/6 Isometrics for Athletes" for power, speed & strength).
- **Plyometrics:** broad jumps, squat jumps, **pogo hops**, **hurdle hops** ("extremely bouncy off the
  ground & get height").
- **Sprints:** short accelerations — e.g. **2-yard build into a 10-yard sprint**; resisted acceleration
  (sled); timed sprints; max-velocity days with plyo drills.
- **FRC / CARs** (controlled articular rotations) in the daily warm-up.

## 5. How to assess [Bergles + Applied]

**His tools at JJ Pearce:** Just Jump mat (vertical jump), **Freelap** timing (sprints),
**MuscleLab contact grid** (jumps/reactive strength). Big data set of sprint times, verticals,
training logs from 9–18 year olds.

**His assessment approach for big groups (FRC with teams):** **Assess → Warm-up → Take-home program.**
Screen mobility/joint ranges, put the common needs into the team warm-up, give individuals with
specific limitations extra work in optional sessions / at home.

**Practical test battery [Applied, using his tools/priorities]:**

| Quality | Test | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Power | Vertical jump (CMJ), standing broad jump | Best of 3. His headline metric. |
| Reactive | Pogo hops / 10-5 RSI on contact mat | Contact time + height. |
| Acceleration | 10 yd (from 2-yd build or standing) | Timing gates or phone app. |
| Max speed | Flying 10 yd (20–30 yd build) | For 13+. |
| Strength | Front squat depth/technique → rep-max for older kids; chin-up max reps | Technique gate before loading. |
| Mobility | Deep squat, hip/shoulder CARs, ankle dorsiflexion, Cossack depth | Feeds warm-up + take-home work. |

Test every ~6–8 weeks. **Athletes log their own numbers** — this builds intent and ownership.

## 6. Best way to improve athleticism (his priorities ranked) [Bergles]

1. **Sprint** — often, short, fast, full intent. Acceleration first; add max velocity with age.
2. **Jump** — plyometrics every speed session (bouncy, stiff, quality contacts).
3. **Get strong through full range** — deep squats, chin-ups, split squats, isometrics.
4. **Mobility / joint health** daily (FRC/CARs) so the body tolerates the work.
5. **Measure to create intent** — timed/measured reps make kids try harder.
6. **Consistency over years** — the real secret.

## 7. Session structure [Bergles + Applied]

He builds the whole session around time/space limits (e.g. a 20–30 min window to rotate between
weight room, sections and turf). Typical flow:

1. **In-place FRC warm-up (8–10 min)** — every day before speed & plyo work. CARs for neck, shoulders,
   spine, hips, knees, ankles + dynamic prep.
2. **Plyometrics (5–10 min)** — pogos, hurdle hops, broad/squat jumps. Low volume, high quality.
3. **Speed (10–15 min)** — 2-yd build into 10-yd sprints, resisted accels, or max-velocity build-ups.
   Full rest. Timed when possible.
4. **Strength (20–30 min)** — 2–4 main movements + isometrics.
5. **(Optional) Mobility take-home / individual needs.**

**Large-group coaching tricks [Bergles]:**
- Stations (jump station / timed sprint station / resisted sprint station) — not everyone can use
  the gear at once, so rotate.
- **Short rotations** keep engagement high.
- **Sightlines matter** — open space with no dividers = lost attention. Organize the room.
- **Athletes coach each other:** "I can't coach 30 people at one time" — peer coaching improves execution.
- Turn boring stuff (mobility, FRC) into routine/competition so 80 kids can do it at once.

## 8. Sample programs [Applied — built on his principles]

### A. Ages 8–10 (2x/week, 45 min)
- Warm-up games (tag, animal crawls, skips) – 10 min
- CARs (hips, shoulders, ankles) – 5 min
- Jumps: pogos 3x10, broad jump 3x3 (stick landing) – 5 min
- Sprints/races: 4–6 x 10 yd (from different starts: lying, kneeling, falling) – 10 min
- Strength circuit 2 rounds: bodyweight squat x10, split-squat hold 20s/side, push-up x5–10,
  flexed-arm hang max, bear crawl 10 yd – 10 min
- Finish with a game – 5 min

### B. Ages 11–14 (3x/week)
**Day 1 – Acceleration + Lower**
- FRC warm-up; pogos 3x15, hurdle hops 3x5
- 2-yd build → 10-yd sprint x 6 (timed), sled push 4 x 10 yd
- Goblet/front squat 3x5 (full depth), FFE split squat 3x6/side, Cossack hold 3x20s/side, plank

**Day 2 – Upper + Isometrics**
- FRC warm-up; med-ball throws 3x5
- Chin-up progression (eccentric 5s x 3–5, 3 sets), push-up 3x8–12, DB row 3x10
- Isometric split-squat & wall-sit holds 3x30s, carries

**Day 3 – Max velocity + Hinge**
- FRC warm-up; A-skips, bounds 3x20 yd, broad jumps 3x3
- Flying 10s x 4 (20-yd build)
- RDL / trap-bar 3x5, single-leg RDL 3x6, Copenhagen/side plank

### C. Ages 15–18 (3–4x/week)
- Same skeleton; progress front squat toward 1.5–2x BW over years, full chin-ups (add weight),
  depth jumps / hurdle hops, sled sprints, flying sprints, test every 6–8 weeks.

**Progression rule:** technique → full range → add reps → add load. Never sacrifice depth for weight.

## 9. Kids-specific do's and don'ts [Bergles]

- ✅ Make it fun; use games and competition; measure things kids care about (speed, jump).
- ✅ Teach the fundamentals early so buy-in lasts years.
- ✅ Isometrics/eccentrics are safe, effective entry points even at 8.
- ✅ Respect overpacked sport schedules — consistent, quality short sessions > big sessions.
- ❌ Don't chase short-term results or train 12-year-olds like 18-year-olds.
- ❌ No half squats, no ego lifting, no rushing to load.
- ❌ Don't let long lines and big unstructured spaces kill engagement.
